IKEv1 vs IKEv2

IKE (Internet Key Exchange) is how two IPsec peers actually agree on and exchange keys before AH or ESP can protect a single packet, and IKEv2 does it in noticeably fewer messages.

Two phases, in both versions

AH and ESP both need shared keys to work with, and IKE is the negotiation that produces them, in two phases:

  • Phase 1: establish a protected channel between the two peers, called the IKE SA (Security Association), that the rest of the negotiation can safely happen over.
  • Phase 2: use that protected channel to negotiate the actual keys AH/ESP will use to protect real traffic.

IKEv1: Main mode or aggressive mode

IKEv1's Phase 1 comes in two variants. Main mode takes six messages and never exposes either peer's identity in cleartext along the way. Aggressive mode collapses that down to three messages, faster, but at the cost of exposing identity information openly, making it the less secure option.

IKEv2: Simplified, and more capable

IKEv2 replaced that split entirely with one fixed exchange: four messages total for the initial setup, no separate main/aggressive choice to make. It also added several things IKEv1 never had: built-in NAT traversal detection, built-in reliability with sequence numbers and retransmission (IKEv1 had no such mechanism of its own), and MOBIKE, which lets a VPN session survive a device switching networks, like moving from WiFi to cellular, without tearing the tunnel down.

WHY MOST NEW DEPLOYMENTS DEFAULT TO IKEV2

Fewer messages, no risky aggressive-mode shortcut, built-in NAT traversal, and mobility support add up to a simpler, more reliable protocol with fewer ways to misconfigure it. IKEv1 still shows up in older or legacy equipment, but IKEv2 is the default choice for anything new.
