DNS Resolution Flow

One question from you turns into a chain of increasingly specific questions someone else asks on your behalf, walking the DNS (Domain Name System) hierarchy one level at a time.

A concierge who does the legwork for you

A recursive resolver, typically your ISP's (Internet Service Provider's) or a public one like 8.8.8.8, acts like a concierge. You ask them one question and walk away; they go ask a chain of increasingly specific direction-givers, root, then TLD (Top-Level Domain), then authoritative, until they actually have the answer, then bring the whole thing back to you as a single reply. You never talk to root or TLD servers directly. The resolver does.

That distinction has a name. Your query to the resolver is recursive: you're asking it to fully resolve the name and hand you a final answer, not a partial one. Every query the resolver then sends onward is iterative: each server it asks is allowed to reply "I don't know the final answer, but here's who to ask next" instead of doing any of the legwork itself.

StepWho asksWho answersQuery type
1ClientRecursive resolverRecursive: do the whole job for me
2ResolverRoot serverIterative: "who handles .com?"
3ResolverTLD serverIterative: "who's authoritative for example.com?"
4ResolverAuthoritative serverIterative: "what's the A record?"
5ResolverClientFinal answer, now cached
WHY MOST QUERIES NEVER ACTUALLY REACH THE ROOT

Resolvers cache answers for as long as each record's TTL (Time to Live) allows, and root/TLD server addresses in particular barely ever change, so they get cached hard. In practice, the vast majority of real-world lookups are answered straight from a resolver's cache after step 1, and the full five-step walk in the diagram above only happens for a genuinely cold, uncached name.
