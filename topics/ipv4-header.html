IPv4 Header Structure

Every router between a packet and its destination reads exactly these fields, and nothing else, to decide what to do with it.

The header, field by field

A router doesn't care what's inside a packet's payload, HTTP request or DNS query or anything else. It only reads the IPv4 header wrapped around that payload, and mostly just a handful of fields in it.

The fields that actually drive behavior

  • TTL (Time to Live): starts at some value (commonly 64 or 128) and is decremented by every router the packet passes through. Hit zero, and the packet is dropped, with an ICMP Time Exceeded message sent back — this single field is the entire mechanism behind traceroute, covered in the next topic.
  • Protocol: tells the receiving host what's riding inside the payload — 6 means TCP, 17 means UDP, 1 means ICMP is next.
  • Source / Destination IP Address: what routing decisions are actually based on — the destination address gets looked up against the routing table at every hop.
  • Flags / Fragment Offset: handle splitting a packet that's too large for a link's MTU into smaller fragments, and reassembling them at the far end.
  • Header Checksum: a simple integrity check on the header itself (not the payload) — recalculated at every hop since TTL changes each time.
WHY THIS FIELD LIST MATTERS BEYOND TRIVIA

When you open a packet capture in Wireshark, this is exactly the breakdown you'll see for every IPv4 packet, field for field. Knowing what TTL and Protocol mean here is what makes a capture readable instead of just a wall of hex.
